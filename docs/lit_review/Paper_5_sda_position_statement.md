# Paper 5: Sports Dietitians Australia Position Statement – Nutrition for Exercise in Hot Environments

# Citation

McCubbin AJ, Allanson BA, Caldwell Odgers JN, et al. Sports Dietitians Australia Position Statement: Nutrition for Exercise in Hot Environments. International Journal of Sport Nutrition and Exercise Metabolism. 2020.

# Purpose

This position statement summarizes evidence-based recommendations regarding hydration, nutrition, cooling strategies, and heat acclimatization for athletes exercising in hot environments.

# Key Findings

# Hydration

* Athletes should begin exercise in a euhydrated state.
* Fluid replacement strategies should be individualized.
* Both dehydration and excessive fluid intake may negatively affect health and performance.
* Monitoring hydration status before exercise is important.

# Electrolyte Replacement

* Electrolyte losses vary substantially between athletes.
* Sodium replacement may assist fluid retention and hydration recovery.
* Electrolyte strategies should be individualized.

# Heat Acclimatization

* Heat acclimation typically occurs over 7–14 days.
* Adaptations include improved thermal regulation, expanded plasma volume, and improved tolerance to heat.
* Heat acclimatization is considered one of the most effective preparations for exercising in hot environments.

# Cooling Strategies

* Cold beverages and ice-slurry drinks may reduce thermal strain.
* Internal cooling strategies can improve comfort during exercise.
* Cooling methods may be used before and during exercise.

# Gastrointestinal Symptoms

* Heat stress may impair gastrointestinal function.
* Elevated core temperatures can increase gastrointestinal discomfort.
* Gastrointestinal symptoms may interfere with hydration and nutrition goals.

# Recovery

* Recovery should include restoration of hydration status.
* Food and beverages can support both hydration and nutritional recovery.
* Recovery plans should consider fluid, electrolyte, and energy needs.

# Warning Signs of Heat Illness

Heat-related illness may progress from:

* Heat exhaustion
* Heat injury
* Heat stroke

Severe symptoms may include:

* Confusion
* Altered mental status
* Collapse
* Seizures

These symptoms require immediate medical attention.
